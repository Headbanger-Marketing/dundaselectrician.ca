A symptom is a starting point, not a diagnosis

Loss of power, flickering, recurring protective-device trips or a damaged device can have different causes. The useful information is what happened, where and when, not a guess made from equipment age. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess the installation within a defined diagnostic scope and explain the identified cause before repair work is approved.

Prepare the scope for your Dundas property

Jot down what you saw on arrival and which rooms are affected, and whether any device shows scorch marks, gives off an odd smell or hums. Mention any past repairs you have heard about, however informal.

Ask the contractor which parts of the property to avoid until the visit, and what should stay switched off until then. Ask how the scope will be worded if nobody can say what earlier repairs involved, and who will explain it to you.

What should the assessment establish?

What differs now from when the empty Dundas property was last in use? The contractor lists the circuits involved and marks each finding as observed or merely suspected, while gaps in the repair history appear openly as limits on how firm any conclusion can be. What caused the trouble, and what repair is proposed? The report gives the cause and describes the repair; insist on both before a repair is approved. Which equipment should stay switched off in the meantime? Each such item is named in the written result.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Should I switch things back on after the home sat empty?

Not without advice: describe what you found to the contractor first and ask what may be switched on. If any device shows heat, a smell, buzzing or sparking, stop using it, leave it alone and never keep resetting a breaker.

Can earlier repairs be the reason something fails now?

Possibly, but a guess is not an assessment. Tell the contractor whatever you know about past repairs, and expect the report to separate points that were confirmed from points that were assumed.
