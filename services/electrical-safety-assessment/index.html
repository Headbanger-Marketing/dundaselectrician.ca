A useful assessment has a stated purpose and boundary

A condition concern, renovation decision or insurer request can each call for a different assessment. Agree which equipment and areas will be reviewed, whether testing is included and what written result will be provided. Concealed wiring and inaccessible equipment may limit conclusions. A general visit should not be represented as complete certification of everything in the property.

Prepare the scope for your Dundas property

Gather seller or buyer papers, earlier inspection reports, ESA paperwork and invoices for past work, then list every alteration you can recall. Mark any area the contractor will not be able to reach, such as a locked room.

Ask what the written result will say about the parts of the property that could not be seen, and whether it may be passed to a buyer or agent. Ask which findings, if any, would need another visit.

What should the assessment establish?

Whether to go ahead with any proposed work before the Dundas home is listed, and when, is the homeowner's decision, and the report supports it. The assessment's purpose, scope and testing are written down before the visit. Hidden wiring from past alterations can restrict every finding, so the contractor states plainly whatever stayed out of view. Observations sit apart from opinions, and each item needing further assessment has its own line. Nothing in the report should imply that previous work is approved or can be made acceptable afterwards. The author, the date, and the equipment and areas covered all appear on it.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Will an electrical review before a sale find everything?

No review can promise that. Wiring behind finished walls and equipment that cannot be reached limit what any visit can report, so ask for those limits to be stated in writing next to the findings.

What should I do with an old repair I cannot find paperwork for?

Tell the contractor exactly what you know and what you only assume, and avoid blaming anyone. The written findings can record that paperwork is missing and what that leaves uncertain.
