Before work begins

Learn what earlier work exists before you build on it.

Past electrical changes can go unrecorded. Behind one wall of an older home altered over the years, wiring of different ages and methods may sit together, and nobody may remember who added a circuit or when.

Gather any old invoices, permit papers, inspection reports and seller disclosures, and photograph the closed panel and its door labels from the outside. Write down every alteration you know of, including who did it, so the contractor starts from facts.

What can I gather when nobody kept records of past work?

Collect invoices, permit papers, earlier ESA paperwork, inspection reports and any labels or notes left near the panel. Where nothing exists, a written list of what you remember is still useful to the contractor.

Should I replace old wiring before adding a new circuit?

That is a decision for the assessment, not a guess. Ask the contractor to explain what the existing wiring appears to be, what can be confirmed without opening walls, and which parts the proposal would rely on.

Which assumptions should quotes state about hidden earlier wiring?

Ask each contractor what was assumed about wiring that cannot be seen. Quotes resting on different assumptions are not like for like, so compare written scope, stated limits and the plan for unexpected discoveries.

Who has to agree before earlier alterations are changed?

Check who owns the property, whether a lender or insurer has conditions and whether a building permit applies. The contractor handles the ESA notification, but you should confirm who can authorize access and sign for changes.

Who actually arranges the work once the form is sent?

Look to a provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor. Treat these pages as background; a sent form arrives for review as an enquiry, but arranges no visit or price and no electrical work. Before agreeing to anything, have the contractor identify itself and its licence, and check it with ESA. Settle the job and its cost and timing directly.
